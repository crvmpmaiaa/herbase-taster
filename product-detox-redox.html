What is on the inside

Three named, and a gap

The listing names three botanicals and says the formula runs to over twenty herbs. It does not name the other herbs, and it does not say which part of any plant is used. Here are the three that are named, with the botany and where the plant grows. The fourth card is the gap, written down rather than filled in. Where a picture on this page is a generated scene rather than a photograph, it says so.

Gigartinaceae · North Atlantic

Chondrus crispusIrish moss

A red alga of cold Atlantic rock, on the European and the North American shore. It grows in flat forking fronds thin enough to see light through, and it pales from red-brown towards cream as it dries. It is the same alga as the sea moss on the rest of this shelf.

Bignoniaceae · South America

Tabebuia impetiginosaPau d'arco

A forest tree of Brazil, Paraguay and northern Argentina that flowers pink before it comes into leaf. The bark is the part usually sold, under the names pau d'arco or lapacho. Newer floras file it under Handroanthus impetiginosus, which is the same tree renamed. Which part of it is in this jar is not printed.

Cucurbitaceae · Asia and the Caribbean

Momordica charantiaBitter melon

A climbing gourd grown across South Asia, southern China and the Caribbean, and cooked as a vegetable in all three. It is picked green for the kitchen, and the one in the picture was left on the vine until it ripened orange. It is about the most bitter thing anybody eats on purpose.

Reiss's turn

Why this page is mostly gaps

Three ingredients are named on the listing for this jar and the rest are not. I could have written this page as though that were normal. It is not normal, and putting it at the top of the sheet is the only honest way to have it on the shelf at all.

A named plant can be checked. Somebody can look up Momordica charantia, see what family it belongs to, ask whether it suits them and decide for themselves. An unnamed one cannot be checked by anybody, and that includes me at the counter. It is the whole argument for printing lists.

There is no serving on the listing either, which is why there is none on this page and no cost per day. I would rather you rang the shop and asked than followed a number I made up to fill a row.

The full list is what this jar needs and it is what it will get. Until then the sheet says so, in the same size type as everything else.

A plant nobody can name is a plant nobody can check.
