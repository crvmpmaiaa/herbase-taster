Two figures for the gel

The Chondrus Crispus listing prints one tablespoon daily. The ceiling, do not exceed one tablespoon a day, is ours: it is in the small print on that page, not on the listing. This box prints one to three.

Sea moss carries iodine and the amount in a spoonful moves with the harvest, so a spoon is not a measured dose in the way a capsule is. That is the reason the lower figure exists, and it is the one on the jar you will be holding.

Who should not take it

Not in pregnancy or while breastfeeding. Not for children.

Anyone with liver disease should leave the Equill jar alone: that is its listing's own advisory, and black cohosh products sold in the UK carry a liver warning as standard. Stop and speak to a doctor if you notice jaundice, dark urine or unexplained abdominal pain. Chasteberry has documented interactions with several classes of prescribed medicine, so if you take anything on prescription, ask your GP or pharmacist before you start rather than after. Anyone with an overactive thyroid should leave the sea moss alone.

Where the box and the jar disagree

This listing prints one to three tablespoons of gel a day. The jar's own listing prints one, and says not to exceed it. Both were written by us, and the one on the jar is the one you will be holding at seven in the morning, so that is the figure I would follow. I have left both on the sheet above rather than quietly deleting the one that makes the box look generous.

The reason the low figure exists is that a spoon of gel is not a measured dose. Seaweed carries iodine and the amount in a spoonful moves with the harvest, which is why the capsules on this site can print a number and none of the gels can. Three spoons of something unmeasured is three times an unknown, and I would rather nobody did that on my say so.
