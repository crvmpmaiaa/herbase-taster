One bark, and the name it is missing

A one ingredient list is a short section. Here is the tree, the part of it in the capsule, what the listing says about where it grows, and the line the listing does not print.

Tree · Fabaceae

AcaciaAcacia bark

Acacia is a genus of thorny trees and shrubs in the pea family, with several hundred species spread across Africa, Arabia, India and Australia. The bark is fibrous and tannic, and bark from trees in this group is a long standing source of tanning material for leather. Milled, it tastes dry in the mouth.

The part used · Bark

Bark, not gumWhat is in the capsule

The listing talks about the resin of the tree, the material sold as gum arabic, and then sells the bark. Those are two different substances off the same kind of tree. Milled bark is what is in this capsule. Gum arabic is not.

Origin · Not printed

A range, not a sourceWhere it is from

The listing gives the tree a range, North Africa through Arabia into India, rather than a country of harvest. No supplier, no region, no species. Until one of those is on the label it is not on this page, and the counter is the place to ask what we know today.

Why this page will not name a species

Acacia is not one tree. It is a genus with hundreds of species across four continents, and the bark of one is not interchangeable with the bark of another. The listing this jar came from names the genus and leaves it there, so that is as far as I can take you.

I could pick the species that is most often meant and print it. Everyone would believe me, and I would have no way of proving it if you asked. A Latin name on a label is a promise about what is in the jar, and I am not making a promise I have not checked with the person who milled it.

So the sheet says not printed, in the row where the answer should be, and it stays there until I have it in writing. If you want to know where that request has got to, ring the shop and ask. It is a short answer either way.

A Latin name on a label is a promise about what is in the jar.
