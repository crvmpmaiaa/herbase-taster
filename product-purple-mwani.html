Red · Rhodophyta

Eucheuma cottoniiPurple mwani

The warm-water species, grown on lines strung between stakes in shallow lagoons off Tanzania. The branches are thick and round in section, closer to a knobbly coral than to the flat forking fans of the Atlantic plant, and the colour runs from pale gold to purple. Sold and farmed as Eucheuma cottonii; botanists more often place the farmed plant in Kappaphycus, as Kappaphycus alvarezii. The trade name is the one on the jar.

Reiss's turn

The cheaper plant, sold as the cheaper plant

Most of what is sold in this country as Irish moss is this species. It is farmed on lines in warm shallow water off Tanzania, Zanzibar, the Philippines and Indonesia, and it costs a fraction of what hand-picked Atlantic Chondrus crispus costs. A good deal of it is then sold at Chondrus prices under a Chondrus name.

So here it is under its own name, at twenty four ninety nine, which is ten pounds under the plain Atlantic jar. Same shelf, different plant, different price. If you have bought sea moss online and it came out pale, stiff and glossy on the spoon, this is most likely what you bought, and you can work out for yourself whether what you paid was the price of this.

Our team went out to the mwani farms in 2020, which is where the word on the jar comes from. The row that annoys me is the volume: the listing says how many days of spoons are in the jar and never how much gel. That is enough to price a day and not enough to compare it with anything else on the shelf, and it is on the list to fix.

It is the cheaper plant, and it is priced like the cheaper plant.
