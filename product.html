What is in it

A Chondrus crispus gel from the cold Atlantic, wild harvested, with the Northern Soul formula blended through it. The formula is four algae from three families: a red, two browns and a green.

Chondrus crispus · Fucus vesiculosus (bladderwrack) · Laminariales (kelp) · Chlorella. Prepared with water and nothing else is added.

How to take it

One tablespoon each morning, on an empty stomach. Straight off the spoon and chased with water, or stirred into a warm tea with raw honey, or blended into a smoothie.

If you miss a morning, do not take two the next day. Seaweed carries iodine, and there is an upper limit on how much of that you want in a day.

Storage

Keep it in the fridge and use it within 28 days of opening. It freezes well if you will not get through it in time. Use a clean, dry spoon and put the lid back on after each serving.

Who should not take it

Anyone with an overactive thyroid should leave it alone. If you take thyroid medication, or you are pregnant or nursing, ask your GP or pharmacist before you start. It is not for children.

We do not print a per-spoon iodine figure for the gel, because it varies with the harvest. If you need to count iodine, Seaking prints 75 µg per capsule.

The routine

How to take it

Step one

Start with a clean, dry spoon. A used one carries bacteria into the jar and shortens its life.

Step two

Take one tablespoon first thing, before you have eaten.

Step three

Follow it with a glass of water. If you would rather not take it neat, stir it into tea with honey or blend it into a smoothie.

Step four

Put the lid on and put it back in the fridge. It keeps for 28 days once opened. Freeze half if that is longer than you need.

What is on the inside

Four algae, three families

Grouped here by colour, which is also how botanists group them. Every one is named in Latin on the jar.

Red · Rhodophyta

Chondrus crispusIrish sea moss

The base of the jar. Picked by hand off cold Atlantic rocks at low tide. This is the plant the old herbals call carrageen.

Brown · Ochrophyta

Fucus vesiculosusBladderwrack

The brown seaweed with the air bladders. It grows on the same shores as Chondrus, a little higher up the beach.

Brown · Ochrophyta

LaminarialesKelp

The largest of the four. It grows in deep-water forests like the one in the photograph, and is dried and milled for the formula.

Green · Chlorophyta

ChlorellaFreshwater green alga

A single-celled green alga. It is cultivated rather than wild, and it is the only one of the four that grows in fresh water.

From Reiss

Why four algae and not one

Plain Chondrus gel is a good thing and I sell it on its own. Northern Soul is the version with the rest of the shore in it. There is a red alga from the low-tide rocks, a brown wrack from a bit higher up the beach, kelp from the deeper water beyond both, and a green freshwater alga to finish.

I group them by colour because the botany does. Those three families separated from each other long before there were land plants, and I like having all of them in one jar. I am not going to tell you what that does for you, because I am not allowed to and I would not want to guess. I can tell you what is in it, and I do, every time.
