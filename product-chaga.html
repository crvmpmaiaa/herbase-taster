What is in it

Inonotus obliquus, the fungus that grows as a hard black mass on living birch. The listing describes what is in the capsule as pure chaga extract and says the formulation is free from fillers and additives.

It does not say which part of the organism the powder is, and it gives no ratio behind the word extract, so neither figure is on this page.

How to take it

Two capsules a day, with breakfast, and at least 500 ml of water.

Two is the serving. If you miss a day, take two the next morning rather than four.

What is not printed on the label

The number of capsules in a jar, which is why there is no supply figure and no cost a day here. The extraction ratio, which is what the word extract would otherwise be telling you. The part used, and the country.

Storage

Who should not take it

Anyone who reacts to mushrooms. Chaga is also one of the botanicals routinely flagged for oxalate content, so if you have had kidney stones, or you have kidney disease, do not start it without asking your GP.

The listing asks anyone using insulin or another medicine for blood sugar to watch their blood glucose closely. We would rather you spoke to your GP or pharmacist before you start at all. Not for children, and ask first if you are pregnant or nursing.

What is on the inside

One fungus, and two blanks

The listing names one ingredient, calls it an extract and gives a milligram figure. Here is the organism, what that word extract is doing, and the shell it goes into.

Fungus · Hymenochaetaceae

Inonotus obliquusChaga

A fungus of living birch. What you see on the trunk is a hard black mass, cracked like charcoal outside and rust orange inside. Botanists call that mass a sclerotium rather than a fruiting body: the fungus makes its fruiting layer later, under the bark, usually once the tree has died.

Prepared · Called an extract

750 mg a capsuleNo ratio printed

An extract is made by pulling something out of the fungus with hot water or alcohol and drying what comes off, and the ratio says how much fungus went in for each gram that came out. Our listing says extract and prints no ratio, so 750 mg is the weight of the powder and not a measure of what stands behind it. The label does not name the part used either.

The shell · Vegan

The capsuleNo gelatin

The powder goes into a vegan capsule. The listing says the formulation is free from fillers and additives, and it does not name the shell material, so neither does the sheet below. The capsules in the picture are a generated scene rather than a photograph of ours.

Reiss's turn

The word extract, and what it is doing on this label

Extract is the most load bearing word on this jar and the label does not define it. You make one by pulling something out of the fungus with hot water or with alcohol, then drying what comes off. How much fungus went in for each gram that came out is the ratio, and the ratio is most of the information.

Ours does not print one. So 750 mg tells you the weight of the powder in the capsule and nothing about what is behind it. That is not unusual and it is not good enough, and the sheet above says so in the row where the number should be.

The other thing missing is the count. Without it I cannot tell you what a day costs, so I do not. Ring the shop and somebody will count a jar out on the counter for you before you buy it.

750 mg tells you the weight of the powder. The ratio would tell you what is behind it.
