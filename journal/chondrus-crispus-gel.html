Sea moss · Product notes

One alga, water, and a drop of oil.

The plain Chondrus crispus gel has three things in it and we can name all three. This is what is in the jar, how it differs from Northern Soul, why there is an oil in a seaweed gel, what people do with it on their skin and hair, and why it gets 28 days rather than a shelf life.

By Reiss Davies Ausar6 September 20268 minute read

Both jars this article compares are paused. Environmental Health has not yet certified the premises the gel is made in, so neither this jar nor Northern Soul is on the shelf right now. Not gone, paused, and everything below still holds true for when they are back. Seaking, the capsule form of the same base plant, is not affected and is on the shelf now.

Two of the jars in our fridge are made out of the same plant. Northern Soul is the one with the rest of the shore in it, four algae from three families, and it was the jar most people walked out holding. The other one is this: Chondrus crispus and nothing else, prepared with water, with a drop of Omega DHA oil through it.

People ask us which of the two they should be having, and the honest answer is that it comes down to how much you want in a jar, which is a question about you and not about seaweed. This article is about the plain one, because it is the jar we get the most questions about and the one with the least written about it anywhere.

01What is actually in the jar

Three things.

  • Chondrus crispus. The cold-water red alga, wild harvested off Atlantic rock at low tide, by hand, in season. Flat forking fronds thin enough to sea light through, drying anywhere from purple to near black. This is the plant the old herbals mean when they write carrageen, and it is the original Irish moss.
  • Water. The seaweed is soaked in it and blended with it. Nothing is taken out and nothing is put back in. Nu is the oldest deity there is, the deification of the primordial watery abyss, the waters from which creation began, and it is still the second ingredient on a jar in a fridge in Liverpool.
  • Omega DHA oil. The same oil we sell in a bottle at £19.99, blended at six parts omega 3 to one part omega 6.

There is no preservative, no sweetener, and no thickener beyond the seaweed itself, which would be an odd thing to put in anyway, given the seaweed is the reason the gel is thick in the first place. That short list is the entire point of this jar. It is why we keep making it alongside the blended one, and why it will stay on the shelf even in the months when it sells half of what Northern Soul sells.

If you want the whole difference between Chondrus crispus and the warm-water Eucheuma cottonii that most sea moss in this country actually is, that is written out at length in Which sea moss?, along with how to tell the two apart with a bag tipped out on a counter. I will not go through it twice.

Three ingredients is not a marketing position. It is what is in it.

03The oil, and the sentence I am not going to write

There is an oil in a seaweed gel, which is an odd thing to find in a seaweed gel, and you are entitled to ask why it is there.

The reason printed on the old listing is a claim, and it is not one I am permitted to repeat under the rules we trade under here. I could sit down and write you a different reason that sounds plausible and that nobody could ever test, and a great many pages are written exactly that way. I would rather not, so here is what I can tell you instead, and all of it is specification.

  • The oil is Omega DHA Oil. We sell it separately in a bottle at £19.99, and that bottle is a month at a tablespoon a day.
  • It is blended at six parts omega 3 to one part omega 6.
  • The amount of it in a jar of gel is not printed on the listing. So I do not print one either, and you should not read this jar as a way of taking omega oil. If that is what you want, buy the bottle, where the serving is measured.
Why I keep saying what I cannot sayWe run a food business in England. The health claims we are allowed to make are the ones on the Great Britain register, they attach to a named nutrient rather than to a plant, and we have to be able to show the product holds enough of that nutrient to qualify. For a gel made out of a wild harvest we usually cannot show it, so the claim does not get made and the sentence does not get written. That is not modesty, it is the law, and most of the sea moss copy you have read online is breaking it.

04How I take it

Before first meal, upon rising, one tablespoon straight off the spoon, then a glass of water. That is how I take it and I have not changed it in years.

If the texture defeats you, and it does defeat some people, there is no shame in it and no prize for suffering. Stir it into a warm herbal tea with a spoonful of raw honey until it goes, or blend it into a smoothie, where it will thicken things up slightly. It goes into soup as well, blended in at the end rather than boiled.

One spoonful is the serving. If you missed yesterday, do not take two today. Seaweed carries iodine, and iodine is one of the very few things on this shelf where more is a genuine problem rather than simply a waste of your money.

05What people do with it on skin and hair

I need to be careful in this section, so read the next sentence exactly as it is written. What follows is a description of what customers do with this jar. It is not a claim about what the gel does to skin or to hair, and I am not making one.

They use it as a face mask or a hair mask. On for up to an hour, then rinsed off with water only, no soap, and patted dry with a towel rather than rubbed at. That is the whole method, it is the method printed on the listing, and a hueman being has been putting food on their face for about as long as there has been food.

Three things worth saying about that, none of which are on the listing and all of which you would get told across the counter.

  1. It is sold as a food. This jar is not registered as a cosmetic and I am not going to market it as one. You are putting a food on your face, which is an old and reasonable thing to do, and the responsibility for doing it sits with you and not with me.
  2. Try a little on the inside of your wrist first and leave it a day. People react to all sorts of things, and a wrist is easier to live with than a face.
  3. Work from a bowl, never the jar. Spoon out what you need and shut the jar. Anything that has touched skin or hair does not go back in, or you will be pouring the jar away well before the 28 days are up and blaming the seaweed.

If you have a skin condition of any kind, or you are under a dermatologist for anything at all, ask them and not me. I sell seaweed and I have never seen your skin.

06Twenty eight days

The jar gets 28 days in the fridge once it is opened, or the freezer if you know you will not finish it inside that. That is the number on the listing and it is the number we stand behind.

The reason it is 28 days and not a year is sat in the ingredient list further up the page. There is no preservative in it. A wet mineral-bearing gel with nothing added to hold it together is a fresh food, and it wants a fridge, a clean dry spoon and the lid back on within the minute. Why a gel goes thin, sour or fizzy, and the order to check the causes in, is set out in Which sea moss?, and every word of it applies to this jar exactly as it applies to the blended one.

One thing specific to this jar. If you are using it on your face as well, you will get through it faster than a spoon a day, and there is no volume printed on the listing, so I cannot tell you how many days of both it holds and I am not going to invent a figure so that the sentence reads better. Freeze half of it the day it lands if you mean to do both.

07Who should not take it

The same list as every other seaweed on our shelf, because the reason is the iodine and this jar is seaweed.

  • Overactive thyroid. If you have hyperthyroidism, sea moss is not for you.
  • Thyroid medication of any kind. Talk to your GP or pharmacist first. If they are happy for you to take it, the capsules are the better form, because the dose is known.
  • Pregnant or nursing. Iodine needs change in pregnancy. Ask your midwife or GP before you start.
  • Children. It is not for them, so keep it out of their reach.
  • On your skin. Patch test on the wrist first, keep it out of your eyes, and if you have a skin condition, ask the person treating it.

None of this is medical advice and I am not qualified to give any. It is the set of questions I put to somebody across the counter before a jar goes into a bag, and you have just been asked all of them.
