What is on the inside

Two names, one jar

This section usually holds a species and the part of it that goes in the capsule. This one holds a problem instead. The listing names two plants from two genera, so both are set out here, and the jar is credited to neither until the label settles it.

No photographof the growing plant, only of the root

Named in the text · Araliaceae

Panax ginsengKorean ginseng

A slow perennial herb of the woodlands of Korea and north east China, with leaflets in whorls of five and a tight head of red berries in late summer. The fleshy root is the part the trade wants, and it sits in the ground four to six years before it is worth lifting. This is the plant our listing names in its body copy, five times.

The part · Root

The root, driedNot printed on our label

A dried Panax root: pale, forked, heavy for its size. This is roughly what the raw material looks like before milling, if the jar is Panax. Our listing prints no part used at all, so the sheet below does not state one, and this photograph is of the species rather than of anything in our sacks.

Named in the heading · Araliaceae

Eleutherococcus senticosusSiberian ginseng

The other name on the listing, and a different plant. Eleutherococcus is a thorny shrub of the Russian Far East, north east China, Korea and northern Japan, and the root and rhizome are what is sold from it. Same family as Panax, different genus, and not a true ginseng at all.

Reiss's turn

Two plants, one label

The heading on our own listing says Siberian. The body says Panax, five times over. Siberian ginseng is Eleutherococcus senticosus, a thorny shrub out of the Russian Far East. Panax ginseng is a slow woodland herb from Korea and north east China that needs six years in the ground before the root is worth lifting. They share a plant family and very little else that matters at a counter, the price of the raw material included, and that gap is not small.

I could have picked one quietly. Panax is the likelier of the two, because the listing's own compound list names ginsenosides and ginsenosides are Panax compounds. But likelier is not printed. If I put Panax on the sheet I have written a specification out of an inference, and the whole point of a sheet is that you can hold it against the jar. So the row says unsettled, and it will keep saying unsettled until there is a supplier document on the counter that says otherwise.

If you have a jar of this at home already and you want to know which plant you have, ring the shop. I will tell you what I know and what I do not, which is what this page has just done at greater length.
