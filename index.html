From Reiss

What I am not allowed to tell you

I can't tell you what these plants do. Not modesty, and not a solicitor making me write it. A food business here can only repeat a health claim that's already on an approved register, and almost nothing in this shop is on it.

Here's the bit nobody mentions. Around two thousand claims about plants have sat "on hold" since 2012. Not rejected. Nobody ruled on them at all. Fourteen years, the file's still open, and I still can't repeat a word of it.

They didn't say no. They said nothing for fourteen years and called it a decision.

Getting a plant onto that register takes a dossier, a committee and a bill most independent shops will never see the far side of. So what's on there isn't what works. It's what somebody could afford to prove. A molecule with a patent behind it gets a sentence you're allowed to read. A root with four hundred years behind it gets nothing.

I'm not going to pretend that's science. I'm not going to get clever about it either. There are shops that keep the good bits on a server in another country and invite you to click through. There are shops that say it sideways and leave you to fill in the gap. Same game, smaller people. It doesn't even work, because the rules follow the seller, not the server.

So here's what I can do. Every formula in here carries its plants by Latin name, the part used, the milligrams in a capsule, where it was harvested and when to stop. Go and look them up. There are centuries of written record on every one, held by people who don't care whether you buy from me. I'd rather you read them than took my word for it.

You asked what's in the jar. That I'm allowed to answer, and I'll answer it in full every time, whether or not it sells.
