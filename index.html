From Reiss

What I am not allowed to tell you

I'm not going to tell you what these plants do. A food business here can only make a health claim that's authorised on the GB register, or still sat on the on-hold list, and an on-hold one only counts if I can substantiate it properly. I can't meet that bar for the plants in this shop. So I don't make the claims.

Here's the bit nobody mentions. Around two thousand claims about plants have sat "on hold" since 2012. Not rejected. Nobody ruled on them at all. Fourteen years, and the file's still open. I'm allowed to use one, but only if I can back it with generally accepted scientific evidence. For these plants I can't, so I don't.

The list is public. Read it, and make your own mind up about the silence.

Getting a plant onto that register takes a dossier, a committee and a bill most independent shops will never see the far side of. So what's on there isn't what works. It's what somebody could afford to prove. A molecule with a patent behind it gets a sentence you're allowed to read. A root with four hundred years behind it gets nothing.

I'm not going to pretend that's science. I'm not going to get clever about it either. There are shops that keep the good bits on a server in another country and invite you to click through. There are shops that say it sideways and leave you to fill in the gap. Same game, smaller people. It doesn't even work, because the rules follow the seller, not the server.

So here's what I can do. Every formula in here carries its plants by Latin name, the part used, the milligrams in a capsule, where it was harvested and when to stop. That's what I can stand behind, and you'll get all of it, every time. What any of it does in a body isn't something I can evidence to the standard the law sets, so I'm not going to point you somewhere that'll say it for me.

You asked what's in the jar. That I'm allowed to answer, and I'll answer it in full every time, whether or not it sells.
