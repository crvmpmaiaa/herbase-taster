What all three do, because the audit said so

  • Proof in the first screen. 4.8 from 19 Google reviews, the address, the opening hours and a price anchor, all above the fold. The audit's finding was that this proof exists on Google and never reaches the site.
  • The brand states specification, the customer states effect. Every page carries that sentence and then obeys it. No formula makes a health claim anywhere.
  • Botanicals by Latin name. Nine of them, printed. This is the cheapest credibility available and Reiss already knows every one.
  • Reviews attributed and dated. Lisa Skillen, Paul Maguire, Sue. Real, unedited, labelled as customer experience rather than claim.
  • No permanent 10% code. The sitewide discount is gone from all three, per the audit.
  • The consultation is a headline product, not something buried. £120, on the first screen of every direction.
  • The protocol is stated honestly, including "once every four months, no more". That is a reason to trust the shop, not a sale to lose.
  • Nothing invented. Doses, counts, botanicals, hours, dates and quotes are all lifted from herbase.earth or the audit.

One deliberate gap: British Supplements publishes extraction method, extract ratio and whole-herb equivalent. Herbase does not publish those anywhere, so I have not invented them. The spec tables are built only from facts that are already true. Filling those rows in is exactly the conversation to have with Reiss, and it is the strongest thing he could add to his own site.
