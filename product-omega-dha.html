The routine

How to take it

Step one

One tablespoon a day. That is the whole of the printed serving. The listing gives no time of day, so pick one you will keep to. The bottle here is a generated scene, not ours.

Step two

Measure it, do not pour it. A tablespoon is 15 ml. Poured by eye, a tablespoon is whatever the neck of the bottle feels like giving you, and the serving figures on the sheet are written against the spoon.

Step three

Keep it in the fridge. Store in the fridge is the only storage instruction the listing prints, and it is worth following from the day the seal comes off.

Step four

A bottle is a month. The listing puts one bottle at a one month supply at a tablespoon a day. It does not print the volume of the bottle, so a month is the only supply figure on this page.

Reiss's turn

A bottle named for a figure it does not print

This one is called Omega DHA Oil. The listing prints no DHA quantity. What it does print is alpha-linolenic acid, which is the omega 3 you get out of plant seeds, a total of nine grams of omega 3 in a tablespoon, and a ratio of six to one. Those are real numbers and they are all on the sheet. The name is doing something the numbers underneath it are not doing, and I would rather say that on our own page than have you work it out afterwards.

The second gap is the source. No plant is named on this label and no other origin is named either. That is why this is the one thing in the shop without a vegan mark on its sheet: I do not know what is in the bottle well enough to put a mark on it, and a mark I cannot evidence is worse than an empty line. It is also why the allergen row says nothing useful. If that matters to you, ring the counter and I will tell you exactly what the supplier has told me, which at the time of writing is not enough.

The third is the bottle itself. A month at a tablespoon a day comes out at about four hundred and fifty millilitres, and I am not printing four fifty, because nobody has printed it to me. All three of those are label problems rather than product problems, and all three are on the reprint list.

The name is doing something the numbers underneath it are not doing.
