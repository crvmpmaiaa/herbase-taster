The routine

How to take it

Step one

Pick one of the two printed schedules. Two capsules morning and evening for 10 days, or two capsules a day for 20 days. Those are the listing's two and they are alternatives, not a pair to run together.

Step two

At least 500 ml of water each time. The listing prints that volume with every serving on both schedules, and it is the one instruction it repeats.

Step three

Mark the last day before you start. Ten days or twenty. Both schedules have an end printed on them, which is more than most labels of this kind give you.

Lid on, dry shelf, out of the sun. No storage line is printed, so here is the ordinary one. Anywhere dry. The generated scene here, with condensation running down the window, is the one place it should not go.

What is on the inside

One tree, named

One botanical, named in Latin on the listing, and a label that stops there. Here is the tree, the question of which part of it is in the capsule, and the figures that are missing.

Tree · Burseraceae

Bursera simarubaGumbo limbo

A fast growing tree of the Caribbean, southern Florida, Mexico and Central America, at home on limestone and along the edges of dry forest. The bark is smooth and coppery and lifts away in thin papery sheets, which is how you place it at a distance and where its nicknames come from. It is often used as a living fence post, because a cut branch pushed into the ground will take root and grow.

Reiss's turn

Forty capsules, probably

Both schedules on this listing come to forty capsules. Two in the morning and two at night for ten days is forty. Two a day for twenty days is forty. That is not a coincidence and anybody who has ever filled a jar can see what it means. It still does not go on the sheet, because a number I worked out on the back of an envelope is not a number you can hold the jar against.

The rest of what is missing is more ordinary and more irritating. No milligrams. No part of the tree, which matters here more than it does on most labels, because gumbo limbo is traded as bark and as leaf and the two are different material at different prices. At £29.99 this is the top of our single botanical range and it carries one of the thinnest labels on the shelf, which is the wrong way round and is on my list.

The tree itself I will happily talk about all afternoon. You see them across the Caribbean, coppery and peeling, and people push cut branches into the ground as fence posts because the branches root and turn into trees. What the capsules do once you have swallowed them is not mine to tell you, and I will not guess at it the way I will not guess at the count.

A number worked out on the back of an envelope is not a specification.
