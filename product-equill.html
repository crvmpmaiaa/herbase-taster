Ranunculaceae · eastern North America

Actaea racemosaBlack cohosh

A woodland perennial of the eastern United States and Canada, with a tall white flowering spike that gives it the older name black snakeroot. The rhizome and root are the parts usually sold. Herbals printed before about 2000 file it as Cimicifuga racemosa, which is the same plant renamed. Which part of it is in this jar is not printed. The caution that travels with this plant is on the sheet below.

Fabaceae · Europe and western Asia

Trifolium pratenseRed clover

The pink-purple clover of European pasture and roadside, now naturalised across most of the temperate world and grown as fodder and as a green manure. The flower head is the part usually dried and sold. The listing does not say which part is in this jar.

No photographof the plant we buy

Dioscoreaceae · eastern North America

Dioscorea villosaWild yam

A twining vine of damp woodland from the eastern United States down into Mexico, with heart-shaped leaves and a knotted underground rhizome. The rhizome is the part usually sold. It is not the yam of the greengrocer, which is a different set of Dioscorea species grown as a staple food.

Reiss's turn

Why proprietary blend is a phrase I want off our labels

Three plants are named on this listing and the rest of it sits behind two words. I understand why formulators reach for them. I also think they fail the only test that counts at a counter, which is whether the person holding the jar can go away and find out what is in it.

The three that are named can be looked up by anyone. You can read that Actaea racemosa is a woodland perennial of the eastern United States, find the caution that travels with it, and decide for yourself. There is no equivalent move for a blend.

The serving is printed twice on this listing and the two do not agree. I have put both on the sheet with the arithmetic for each, rather than pick the version that makes the day rate look better. Ring the shop and somebody will read you what the current label says.

The caution on this one is not a formality. Black cohosh carries a warning about the liver wherever it is sold here, and the listing's own advisory says the same thing. It sits above the small print on this page rather than under it, because that is where people actually read.

Three names and a blank is not an ingredient list.
