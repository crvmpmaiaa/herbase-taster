Tree · Bignoniaceae

Tabebuia impetiginosaPau d'arco

A forest tree of Brazil, Paraguay and northern Argentina that flowers pink on bare branches before it comes into leaf, which is what the photograph shows. The bark is what the trade takes, under the names pau d'arco, ipe roxo and lapacho. It is the same tree that opens the ingredient list of Decolonise.

Reiss's turn

The bark, and the name it lost

This jar says Tabebuia impetiginosa and the botanists say Handroanthus impetiginosus. The genus was split in 2007 and a large part of Tabebuia moved across, but the trade never followed, so the old name is still what you will find on nearly every sack of this bark in the world. Both names are the same tree. I print both, because you should be able to look it up under either one and land in the same place.

What I like about this label is that it says inner bark. That sounds like nothing. It is the one thing most bark labels leave out, and it is the difference between the living layer just under the surface, which is what the trade actually asks for, and whatever came off the outside of the log. Several other jars on this shelf will not tell you which part of the plant is in them. This one does, and I would rather praise a label for that than pretend it is normal.

What it does not tell you is how many capsules are in the jar, how many milligrams are in each of them, or how long a course is meant to run. So there is no cost a day here, and if you are thinking about taking it for months rather than weeks, ring the counter and ask, because the label will not answer you and neither will this page.
