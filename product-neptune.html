A small tropical tree of the Caribbean and of Central and South America, now grown across the tropics, carrying a heavy green fruit covered in soft curved spines. Two different plants are sold under this name. Matricaria chamomilla, called German chamomile or scented mayweed, is an annual of European field edges and disturbed ground. Chamaemelum nobile, Roman chamomile, is a low creeping perennial of western Europe. Same family, different genus, different plant. The listing prints the English word and no binomial, so we print no binomial either. The dried flower head is the part sold in both cases.

Caprifoliaceae · Europe and western Asia

Valeriana officinalisValerian

A tall perennial of damp ground and riverbank through Europe and into western Asia, with divided leaves and a flat head of pale pink flowers. The root and rhizome are the parts usually sold, and they carry a smell that everybody notices and few people enjoy. The listing prints the common name only; this is the species the word covers in the trade. Older books put the genus in a family of its own, Valerianaceae, since folded into Caprifoliaceae. The caution that travels with this plant is on the sheet below.

Verbenaceae · North America

Verbena hastataBlue vervain

A tall perennial of wet meadow, ditch and streamside across North America, with narrow toothed leaves and candelabra spikes of small blue-purple flowers that open in a ring working upward. The name blue vervain belongs to this species; plain vervain usually means Verbena officinalis, a different European plant entirely. The aerial parts are what is usually gathered. Reiss's turn

Why chamomile is not a species

Chamomile is on this listing with nothing after it, and that is not a small omission. Two different plants are sold under the word. Matricaria chamomilla is an annual of European field edges. Chamaemelum nobile is a low creeping perennial. Same family, different genus, and no way at all to tell from a label that just says chamomile.

I could pick one. Most people writing a page like this would take the commoner of the two, put it in italics, and nobody would ever query it. I do not know which is in the jar, and a Latin name invented for a label is worse than an English one printed honestly.

The storage line on the listing says to store it away from radiation. I cannot act on that and neither can you, so the sheet above carries the plain instruction instead and says what it replaced. I would rather show you the edit than make it quietly.

The valerian warning is standard on every valerian product sold in this country and it is on the sheet for the same reason the stop date is on Decolonise. A limit printed plainly is worth more to somebody at a counter than a paragraph of reassurance.

A Latin name invented for a label is worse than an English one printed honestly.
