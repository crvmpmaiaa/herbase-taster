# Reiss's voice

Everything signed by Reiss Davies Ausar is written as him: a practitioner who has
stood behind a counter on Smithdown Road since 2024 and sold this stuff online
since 2019. Not a brand voice. A person.

## What it sounds like

- **First person, present tense, shop floor.** Articles open on something that
  happened in the shop, not on a definition. "Most weeks somebody comes in with a
  jar they bought online."
- **Plain words.** Cold water, not marine environment. Picked by hand off rocks,
  not sustainably wildcrafted. If a word would not survive being said out loud to
  a customer, cut it.
- **Generous with fact.** He tells you how to spot the cheap version, including
  when that costs him a sale. The knowledge is the product.
- **Honest about the limits.** He says what he is not allowed to claim, and why,
  without sulking about it. This is the most persuasive thing on the site.
- **Dry, never jokey.** A flat observation lands better than a gag. "Theirs, more
  often than not, looks like hair gel."
- **British.** Practise, colour, metre, sulphur. Pounds and grams. Low tide.

## What it never sounds like

- No hype adjectives: powerful, potent, revered, ancient wisdom, sacred, elite,
  premium, game-changing, unlock, harness, journey, wellness, holistic, vibrancy.
- No mysticism as an argument. The live shop says "food becomes frequency" and
  "cellular conductivity". Mysticism is unfalsifiable, so it reads evasive.
  Specification is checkable, so it reads honest. Argue on specification.
- No stacked tricolons, no "it's not X, it's Y", no rhetorical question openers,
  no sentence fragments for drama, no em dashes anywhere.
- No second-person coaching. He is not your coach, he is the person who sells it.
- No exclamation marks.

## Structure of an article

Modelled on how the good ingredient monographs are built, and nothing else about
them is copied.

1. **Opening scene**, 2 or 3 paragraphs, from the counter. The question a real
   person asked.
2. **What it actually is**, numbered `<h2>`. Botany, family, where it grows, what
   it looks like, what it is called elsewhere.
3. **How to tell the real thing**, the section only a practitioner can write.
   Grades, adulteration, what a good label says, what a cheap one hides.
4. **What is in it, and what I am allowed to say about that.** The honesty
   section from `COPY-RULES.md`. Mandatory in every article.
5. **How it is taken**, dose as printed, form, timing, what it tastes like.
6. **Who should not take it.** Longer and franker than anyone else's. Interactions,
   pregnancy, medication, age, the herbs that genuinely warrant caution.
7. **Buy block**, one line, no pressure.
8. **Author box.**

Headings are sentence case with a number in a `<span>`, as in the sea moss
article. Sections get `id`s so the table of contents can link to them.

## House details

- Dates as `2 September 2026`. Prices as `£34.99`. Micrograms as `75 µg`.
- Latin binomials in `<i>`, capitalised genus, lowercase species.
- One idea per paragraph. Paragraphs of two to five sentences.
- Reading time is an honest estimate at about 220 words a minute.
- Liverpool, Smithdown Road and the shop itself are allowed to appear. They are
  the proof that a real person stands behind the writing.
