Hericium erinaceus, the shaggy white bracket fungus known as lion's mane, encapsulated on its own with no fillers. The listing doesn't yet print a capsule count, a capsule weight or an extraction method, so this page states only what is confirmed and queries the rest.

Hericium erinaceus grows on the wounds of hardwood trees, mostly beech and oak, in temperate forests across the northern hemisphere. It fruits as a single cascading mass of long white spines rather than a cap and stem, which is where its common names come from: lion's mane, bearded tooth, monkey head, pom pom mushroom.

Herbase's capsules are described on the listing as 100% Hericium erinaceus with no fillers, bulking agents or contaminants. What isn't stated is whether the mushroom used is fruiting body, grown on logs or substrate and harvested as the actual fungus, or mycelium grown on grain, which is a cheaper and very different material sold under the same name. Neither is disclosed on the current listing, and it's the single most useful thing this label could say.

The same goes for extraction. A capsule can be filled with plain dried powder, or with a hot water or dual extract concentrated to a stated ratio, and the two behave very differently. None of that is printed here either. A beta-glucan percentage, the marker most labels use to show how much of the mushroom's structural material actually made it into the capsule, is also missing.
