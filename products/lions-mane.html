Hericium erinaceus - the shaggy white bracket fungus known as lion's mane, encapsulated here on its own, no fillers added. The current listing doesn't print a capsule count, a capsule weight or an extraction method, so this page holds to what's confirmed and leaves the rest as a question.

Hericium erinaceus grows out of the wounds of hardwood trees, mostly beech and oak, right across the temperate forests of the northern hemisphere. It doesn't fruit as a cap and stem at all, it fruits as one cascading mass of long white spines, and that's where every one of its names comes from: lion's mane, bearded tooth, monkey head, pom pom mushroom.

The listing describes Herbase's capsules as 100% Hericium erinaceus, no fillers, no bulking agents, no contaminants. What it doesn't say is whether the mushroom used is fruiting body, the fungus itself, grown on logs or substrate and harvested directly, or mycelium grown on grain, a cheaper and very different material that gets sold under the same name. Neither is stated on the current listing, and it's the one fact that would tell a customer what they're actually holding.

The same question sits over extraction. A capsule can be filled with plain dried powder, or with a hot water or dual extract concentrated down to a stated ratio, and the two aren't the same material at all. None of that's printed here either. A beta-glucan percentage, the figure most labels use to show how much of the mushroom's structural material actually made it into the capsule, is missing too.

Two capsules in the morning, with at least 500 ml of spring water.
