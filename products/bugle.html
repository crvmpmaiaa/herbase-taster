Lycopus virginicus, the listing calls it, though the name printed on the front is Bugle, and bugle usually means a different plant entirely. We have asked Reiss to confirm which one is actually in the capsule.

Bugle is a common name with more than one owner. British herbal use gives it to Ajuga reptans, a low, spreading mint-family plant with blue flower spikes, common enough on damp woodland edges. American and continental use gives bugleweed to Lycopus virginicus, or its European relative Lycopus europaeus, mint family too but a different genus altogether, taller, favouring wet ground near streams and ditches.

This listing's title says Bugle. The text underneath says Lycopus Virginicus, calls it Bugleweed throughout, and never once says Ajuga. So Lycopus it is, on the name printed. But a mix-up like this is exactly the kind worth confirming before the page goes live.
