Bugle capsules. The listing names the plant as Lycopus virginicus, a species usually sold under the common name bugleweed rather than bugle, which is more often the name given to a different plant, Ajuga reptans. We have asked Reiss to confirm which one is actually in the capsule.

Bugle is a common name with more than one owner. In British herbal use it usually means Ajuga reptans, a low, spreading plant in the mint family with blue flower spikes, common on damp woodland edges. In American and continental use, bugleweed usually means Lycopus virginicus or its European relative Lycopus europaeus, also mint family plants but a different genus entirely, growing taller and favouring wet ground near streams and ditches.

This listing's title says 'Bugle' but the body text names Lycopus Virginicus and calls the plant 'Bugleweed' throughout, which points to the Lycopus species rather than Ajuga. We have used the name printed in the listing text, but this is exactly the kind of common-name mix-up worth confirming before the page goes live.
