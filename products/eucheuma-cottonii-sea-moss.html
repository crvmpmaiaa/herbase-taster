A purple sea moss gel made from Eucheuma cottonii, a warm-water species farmed off Zanzibar, Tanzania, in the Indian Ocean. Sold in a single jar size.

Mwani is Swahili for sea moss. Eucheuma cottonii is the species most often sold under that name, and it is a different plant to the cold-water Chondrus crispus the rest of this shelf is built from. It grows in warm, shallow Indian Ocean water and is farmed on lines rather than picked off cold-water rock, which is part of why it costs less than the Atlantic jars and why the colour runs to a deeper purple.

As with the other gels on this site, there is no printed iodine figure for Purple Mwani, so no iodine claim is made about it here. If you want a measured number, Seaking prints 75 µg of iodine per capsule.

Use a clean spoon and take it neat or stirred into a smoothie. One tablespoon a day is roughly a 20 day jar, two tablespoons a day roughly ten.

How it is taken

Before you take it

Who should not take this

Safety information is the one thing a shop is always allowed to tell you, so we tell you all of it.

  • Avoid if pregnant, trying to conceive, or taking any blood-related pharmaceutical medication.
  • Sea moss contains natural iodine, which varies from harvest to harvest. If you take thyroid medication or have a thyroid condition, ask your GP or pharmacist before taking this daily.
  • Food supplements should not be used as a substitute for a varied and balanced diet and a healthy lifestyle. Keep out of reach of young children.
