An oil blend formulated to a stated ratio of six parts omega-3 to one part omega-6, delivering 9,000 mg of omega-3 per tablespoon. The listing doesn't name the plant it's pressed from or confirm a DHA figure, so both sit logged as queries.

The listing describes this oil as rich in alpha-linolenic acid, ALA, an omega-3 fatty acid, blended to a ratio of six parts omega-3 to one part omega-6. It states a figure of 9,000 mg of omega-3 per tablespoon. What it doesn't state anywhere is which plant the oil's pressed from, flaxseed, hemp, chia or something else, and there's no separate DHA figure printed, only the total omega-3 number.

That distinction actually matters. ALA is a precursor the body converts into other fatty acids, DHA among them, but it isn't DHA itself, and a product's DHA content has to be measured and printed on its own before it says anything about DHA specifically. Until we have that figure and the source plant confirmed, this page states the ratio and the total omega-3 figure exactly as printed, and nothing more.
