Ganoderma lucidum - sold here as reishi, 50 vegan capsules at 800 mg each, described on the listing as 100% pure with no fillers.

Ganoderma lucidum is a bracket fungus that grows on the trunks and roots of deciduous trees, plum, oak and maple especially. In the wild it forms a hard, woody, kidney-shaped cap with a glossy, varnished surface, dark reddish-brown fading to cream at the growing edge. It's known across East Asia under many names, most commonly reishi in Japan and lingzhi in China, and few fungi anywhere carry as long a continuous trade history.

Herbase's capsules list the fill as 800 mg of Ganoderma lucidum, described as 100% pure, in a pack of 50 vegan capsules. What the listing doesn't say is whether that 800 mg is the whole mushroom in powdered form, fruiting body or mycelium grown on grain, or a concentrated extract at a stated ratio. It doesn't give a beta-glucan figure either. These are the facts that would actually tell a customer what grade of reishi they're buying, and none of them are on the label yet.
