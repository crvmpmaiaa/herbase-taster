A pyramid cut and polished from shungite, the carbon-bearing stone from the shungite deposits of Karelia in north west Russia. This is a mineral object, not a food supplement: no dose, no ingredients list, just the stone itself, 8cm at the base.

Shungite comes from a group of deposits in Karelia, in north west Russia, close to Lake Onega. The rock formed in the Precambrian, somewhere around two billion years ago, from organic sediment that was buried, heated and compressed until its carbon content locked into place. It is one of the oldest carbon-bearing rocks known, and one of the few places on earth where carbon of this kind occurs in a solid, workable stone rather than as coal or graphite.

The stone itself is black, dense and slightly greasy to the touch when polished, closer in feel to graphite than to a typical crystal. It cuts and takes a shine well, which is why it is worked into pyramids, spheres and jewellery rather than left as raw rubble. This piece is cut to an 8cm x 8cm pyramid, with the four faces polished to a low sheen.

Shungite is also one of the very few natural minerals known to contain fullerenes, carbon molecules including C60, in trace amounts. That is a genuine, documented feature of the rock's chemistry. It is a fact about what the stone is made of, not a description of anything it does once it is in a room.
