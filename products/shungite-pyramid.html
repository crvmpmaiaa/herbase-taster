Shungite - a carbon-bearing stone, cut and polished into an 8cm x 8cm pyramid here. It comes from the Karelia deposits in north west Russia, and it's a stone, not a supplement: no dose, no ingredients list, just the mineral itself, eight centimetres at the base.

Shungite comes out of a group of deposits in Karelia, close to Lake Onega, in the far north west of Russia. The rock formed in the Precambrian, something like two billion years back, laid down as organic sediment then buried, heated and pressed until the carbon in it locked solid. It's one of the oldest carbon-bearing rocks we know of, and one of the few places on earth where that carbon sits in a workable stone, rather than as coal or graphite.

The stone itself is black, dense and a little greasy under the fingers once it's polished, closer to graphite in feel than to a crystal. It cuts and takes a shine well, which is why it gets worked into pyramids, spheres and jewellery rather than left as raw rubble. This piece is an 8cm x 8cm pyramid, four faces polished to a low sheen.

Shungite is also one of the small number of natural minerals known to carry fullerenes, carbon molecules that include C60, in trace amounts. That's a genuine, documented feature of the rock's chemistry. It's a fact about what the stone is made of, not what it does once it's sitting in a room.

Before you take it

Who should not take this

Safety information is the one thing a shop is always allowed to tell you, so we tell you all of it.

  • Not a food supplement, and not for ingestion.
  • Keep it away from small children. A stone this size and weight is a choking and an impact hazard.
  • Polished stone chips if it's dropped on a hard surface. Handle it the way you'd handle any dense mineral piece.

If you take prescribed medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or are under the care of a doctor for anything at all, speak to them before starting a supplement. This is a food shop and not a medical service.
