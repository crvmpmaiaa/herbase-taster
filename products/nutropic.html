Nutropic is sold as vegan capsules, two a day. The listing describes 'wild, mineral-rich plants' and never names a single one, reaching instead for minerals such as gold, silver and palladium, using a term for minerals reduced to single atoms that isn't standard nutritional science. Until Reiss tells us what's actually growing in this capsule, this page stays this short.

This is the thinnest listing in the whole formula range. Most of the shop's blends name at least a handful of their herbs, even where none of the surrounding claims survive, and Nutropic names none. What the copy does give is a list of minerals, gold, silver, iron, copper, selenium, nickel and palladium, described in that same single-atom language, a category nutritional science doesn't recognise, with no figure anywhere for how much of any of them the capsule actually holds. None of it can go in a specification without a number attached.

Until there's an actual ingredient list to work from, this page carries the form, the dose and the safety information the shop already prints, and a plain note that the rest is missing.

Before you take it

Who should not take this

Safety information is the one thing a shop is always allowed to tell you, so we tell you all of it.

  • Not for use in pregnancy or while breastfeeding.
  • Speak to your GP before taking this if you're on any prescribed medication. The formula's ingredients aren't published, so a possible interaction can't be ruled out.
  • If you have a diagnosed medical condition, speak to your GP before taking this rather than treating it yourself.

If you take prescribed medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or are under the care of a doctor for anything at all, speak to them before starting a supplement. This is a food shop and not a medical service.
