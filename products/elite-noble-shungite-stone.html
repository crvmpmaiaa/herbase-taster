A hand-sized piece of noble shungite, the high-carbon grade of the stone, cut from the Karelia deposits in north west Russia. Around 20 to 30 grams, no ingredients, no dose: a mineral object described as what it is.

Shungite from Karelia varies a great deal by grade. Ordinary shungite runs to somewhere between 30 and 50 percent carbon and is dull black. The rarer grade, sometimes called noble or elite shungite, runs much higher, close to pure carbon, and has a silvery, almost metallic lustre where ordinary shungite is matte. This stone is sold as that higher grade.

The listing states a carbon content of around 99 percent for this stone. That figure describes the proportion of the rock that is carbon, which is a straightforward compositional fact about a mineral, the same kind of figure you'd expect for any ore or gemstone. It is not a description of anything the stone does once you own it.

Shungite of any grade, including this one, is also one of the few natural minerals known to contain trace fullerenes, including C60. That's a genuine feature of the rock's chemistry and worth stating plainly. Each stone weighs approximately 20 to 30 grams, small enough to sit in a pocket or a drawer.
