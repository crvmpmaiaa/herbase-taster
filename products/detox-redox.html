Detox Redox is described as a blend of more than twenty different herbs, and the listing names only three of them, sea moss, pau d'arco and bitter melon. The other seventeen or more are never specified, and no dose is printed anywhere on the listing either, and that makes this one of the thinnest pages in the range for one of the biggest claimed formulas.

Sea moss is Chondrus crispus, the same red seaweed the shop sells as a gel under the Northern Soul label, here presumably dried and powdered rather than kept as a gel. Pau d'arco is Tabebuia impetiginosa, a bark harvested from a flowering tree native to Central and South America, long used in Latin American herbal tradition and increasingly common in Western supplement blends too. Bitter melon, Momordica charantia, is a warty, cucumber-shaped fruit from a climbing vine grown across South and Southeast Asia, used in regional cuisines as much as in herbalism.

Beyond these three the listing says the formula holds 'over 20+ different herbs' and stops there. That's somewhere around seventeen plants unaccounted for, in a product the shop sells for £39.99, and it's the single biggest gap on this page, the reason it can't carry a fuller specification.

Before you take it

Who should not take this

Safety information is the one thing a shop is always allowed to tell you, so we tell you all of it.

  • Not for use in pregnancy or while breastfeeding.
  • Speak to your GP before taking this if you're on any prescribed medication. With most of the formula's ingredients unpublished, a possible interaction can't be ruled out.
  • If you have a diagnosed medical condition, speak to your GP before taking this rather than treating it yourself.
  • Do not take alongside other medication without medical advice, as printed on the original listing.

If you take prescribed medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or are under the care of a doctor for anything at all, speak to them before starting a supplement. This is a food shop and not a medical service.
