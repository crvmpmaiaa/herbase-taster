A dual-extracted resin scraped from rock in the Altai Mountains, sold in a 50 g amber glass jar. Fulvic acid and humic acid are named on the listing as compounds present in the resin.

Shilajit is a dark, sticky resin that seeps out of rock strata in a handful of high-altitude mountain regions, the Altai among them. It forms over long stretches of time as plant matter, trapped and compressed between layers of rock, breaks down slowly under pressure and heat into a dense organic material rich in humic substances. It's essentially the compressed by-product of ancient plant decay squeezed out of the mountainside.

Herbase's resin is described as dual-extracted and lab-tested, sold in a 50 g amber glass jar. Fulvic acid and humic acid are the two named compounds on the listing, both naturally occurring humic substances typical of this kind of material rather than anything unique to this product. The listing also names a longer list of minerals present, including zinc, copper, nickel, manganese, potassium, phosphorus, vanadium, chromium, cobalt and magnesium, though it doesn't give a lab-verified breakdown with quantities.

Because shilajit is scraped from rock rather than grown and harvested, purity testing is a real and useful thing to ask about, specifically testing for heavy metals like lead, arsenic and mercury, which can occur alongside the mineral content in unrefined material from some sources. Whether this particular resin has been tested for that isn't stated on the current listing, and it's the single fact that would most improve this page.
