What is on the inside

One shrub, named

One botanical, one Latin name, and a label that stops sooner than it should. Here is the plant, the fruit the common name refers to, and the two figures the listing leaves out.

Shrub · Lamiaceae

Vitex agnus-castusChasteberry

A deciduous shrub or small tree of the Mediterranean and western Asia, at home on riverbanks and on dry stony ground. The leaves are palmate, five to seven narrow leaflets fanning out from one point and grey underneath. In late summer it throws long spikes of small lilac flowers, which is when you can place it from a passing car.

The fruit · The name

The berry itselfPart not printed

The fruit follows the flower: small, hard, dark when ripe, about the size of a peppercorn. That dried fruit is what the trade sells under this name. Reiss's turn

Why this is a short sheet for a well documented plant

This is the page where I have the least to print, and it is not because the plant is obscure. Vitex agnus-castus has been in European herbals for centuries and it is one of the better studied botanicals in the shop. The trouble is the gap between what is known about a plant and what a shop is allowed to write on a shelf card next to a jar of it. Nearly everything I could tell you about this one sits on the far side of that line, so it stays off the page.

What is left is a species, a count, a serving and a set of blanks. Fifty capsules. Two in the morning before breakfast. Twenty five days, then five off. No milligrams, because the label does not carry them. No part of the plant named, because the label does not carry that either. On a herb this well documented, both of those are label problems rather than sourcing problems, and both are on my list for the reprint.

One thing I will say plainly, because it is a caution and not a claim. This is not a herb to start on your own while you are taking something else on prescription. The interactions are documented, the list is not a short one, and a pharmacist can read through it with you in a minute at a counter. Ask first. If the answer comes back no, the twenty five pounds stays in your pocket, and that is the better result of the two.
