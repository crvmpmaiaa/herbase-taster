One plant, and one blank

A one ingredient list is a short section. Here is the plant, what the listing says about where it was grown, the part of it that is not named, and the shell it goes into.

Herb · Petiveriaceae

Petiveria alliaceaAnamu, guinea hen weed

A slender perennial herb of tropical America and the Caribbean, in a small family long grouped with the pokeweeds. Leaf and root smell strongly of garlic when they are crushed, which is where the species name alliacea comes from, and it carries into the breath of anyone taking it.

From Reiss

When the arithmetic on a listing does not add up

This listing says 50 capsules at the top, and then says one a day lasts 40 days and two a day lasts 20. Both of those work out at 40 capsules. One of the three numbers is wrong and I do not yet know which, so all three are on the sheet above with that sentence attached.

It matters because the count is the only figure you can use. There are no milligrams on this label, so you cannot compare it with the jar beside it on strength. What you can do is count what arrives, divide the price by it, and hold me to that.

The other blank is the part of the plant. Root and leaf both get milled and sold under this name, and they are not the same thing in a capsule. When the supplier puts it in writing, the row above changes from not printed to whichever it is.
