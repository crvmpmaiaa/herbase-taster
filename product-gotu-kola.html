What is on the inside

One leaf, and two plants called brahmi

A one ingredient list is a short section. Here is the plant, where it is eaten as food, the row the label leaves empty, and the shell it goes into.

Herb · Apiaceae

Centella asiaticaGotu kola, Indian pennywort

A creeping perennial of damp ground across the tropics and subtropics, in the carrot and parsley family. It puts out runners along the soil and sends up round, scalloped leaves on single stalks. The taste is green and slightly bitter.

Also a food

Not only a capsuleHow it is eaten

In Sri Lanka the raw leaf is shredded into a sambol with coconut and lime. In Vietnam it is blended with water and ice and sold cold on the street. That is worth knowing when you are deciding what a capsule of it is, and it is a fact about kitchens rather than about you.

From Reiss

When the caution is longer than the ingredient list

The ingredient list on this jar is one line. The list of people who should not take it runs to five. That is the honest shape of this product and I would rather you saw it on the way in than found it on the way out, so it is printed above at full length in the words the listing uses.

A long caution is not a mark against a plant. It usually means somebody has bothered to write down what is known, which is more than you get from a jar that says nothing at all. Read it, and if a line applies to you, put the jar back on the shelf. That is what the line is for.

The rest of the label is thin: no count, no milligrams, no part named, no country. Gotu kola is eaten as a leaf in Sri Lanka and drunk cold in Vietnam, so this is not an unfamiliar plant anywhere, and there is no good reason for those four rows to stay empty. They are on my list.
