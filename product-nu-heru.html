What is on the inside

One alga named, three more listed

The listing names a base and three additions, and the phrase it uses in front of them is various ingredients like. That phrase is why this section has four cards and the sheet below has a blank where the declared list should be.

Red · Rhodophyta

Chondrus crispusIrish sea moss

The base. Picked by hand off cold Atlantic rocks at low tide, flat forking fronds that dry dark. This is the plant the old herbals call carrageen, and it is the same base that carries Northern Soul and is sold on its own as the plain gel.

Fruit · Malvaceae

AdansoniaBaobab

The listing says baobab and names no species. There are several trees in the genus and the fruit of more than one is traded, so this card stops at the genus. The fruit is a hard pod with a dry pale pulp inside, and it is that pulp which is powdered.

Berry · Adoxaceae

Sambucus nigraElderberry

The listing says elderberry and gives no Latin name. In this country the word means Sambucus nigra, the black elder of hedgerows and roadsides. The berries are cooked or dried before use and are not eaten raw.

Calyx · Malvaceae

HibiscusHibiscus

The listing says hibiscus and names no species. The hibiscus used as a food is the deep red calyx of Hibiscus sabdariffa, dried, which is what makes the sharp red infusion people know. Our label does not confirm that is the one, so this card stops at the genus.
